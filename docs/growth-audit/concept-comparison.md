# Campaign concept comparison — Day 1/2

Sketches: [Lost & Found](concept-lost-and-found.html) &middot; [Gift Shop](concept-gift-shop.html) &middot; [Departures](concept-departures.html). Each is one sample item, not the full four-entry build (brief §4: "sketches, not three builds").

## Status: judgement-based, not interview-validated

Brief §4 specifies 15-minute sessions with target buyers (heads of ops/transformation, AI adoption owners, agent-sponsoring execs), five contacts to be supplied by Akshat. **No interview subjects were available for this pass** — this is Day 1 solo work, and Akshat's five contacts/introductions haven't been sourced yet. Per the brief's own fallback ("If interviews cannot be arranged, present an explicitly judgement-based comparison and continue independent homepage/tool work while the selection is pending"), the scores below are my judgement only, using the same rubric the brief specifies, so real interview data can slot into the same table later without changing its shape.

**Do not treat this table as validated. It's a starting point for Akshat's Day 2 decision, or for real 15-minute sessions if contacts become available before then.**

## Scoring (1–5, weighted per brief §4)

| Criterion | Weight | Lost & Found | Gift Shop | Departures |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Buyer recognition | 35% | 4 | 4 | 5 |
| Connection to paid work | 30% | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| Willingness to share with a colleague | 20% | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| Build simplicity | 15% | 4 | 3 | 3 |
| **Weighted total** | | **4.15** | **3.60** | **4.05** |

Reasoning, item by item:

**AI Lost & Found — 4.15.** The lost-property format is instantly legible and the tone is the easiest of the three to keep constructive rather than mocking, which matters for a site aimed at buyers, not a general AI-humor audience. Direct line from "item" to "adoption failure" to scorecard is the tightest fit with paid work of the three concepts — every item is a rollout symptom by construction. Simplest to build: four tagged items, one shelf, no extended metaphor to sustain.

**AI Office Gift Shop — 3.60.** Most immediately funny, which the brief itself flags as its main risk: it reads closer to generic "AI hype is silly" content and closest to the CISO-campaign reference article's own gift-shop device, so it risks feeling derivative rather than distinctly Business Backpacker. Connection to paid work is real but one layer more indirect ("what this actually needs" is a fix, not a diagnosis) — scored it a point below Lost & Found on that criterion. Held build simplicity down slightly because product-catalogue framing invites scope creep (packaging, price tags, "add to cart" jokes) that has to be deliberately resisted.

**AI Departures — 4.05.** Airport departures is extremely widely recognized, arguably the most universally legible format of the three, which is why buyer recognition scored highest here. Best aesthetic fit with Business Backpacker's travel identity, per brief. Docked on shareability and build simplicity for the same reason the brief flags: sustaining the flight metaphor accessibly (plain-language status labels alongside "Delayed," a board that still reads correctly to someone unfamiliar with departure boards) takes more care across four rows than a shelf of lost items does.

## Recommendation

**Lost & Found**, matching the brief's own starting hypothesis (§4). It scores highest on the criterion most tied to commercial outcome (connection to paid work) and lowest on build risk, and it's the easiest of the three to keep tonally constructive rather than snarky — relevant given the brief's own framing that the tone must stay constructive. Departures is a close second and worth keeping as the fallback if real interviews surface stronger recognition data for it; it's the best aesthetic fit with the existing travel/backpacker identity, so if Akshat prioritizes brand cohesion over the tightest CTA linkage, it's the reasonable alternative.

**This recommendation is a hypothesis, not a decision.** Per brief §4, Akshat chooses on Day 2. If five buyer contacts become available before then, real 15-minute sessions using the four questions in brief §4 ("What is this?", "Who would you send it to?", "What problem does it remind you of?", "What would you click next?") should run against these three sketches before the choice is finalized — the scores above are a placeholder for that data, not a replacement for it.

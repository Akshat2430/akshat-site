You ran the session. Everyone nodded. Nobody's workflow moved.

The workshop created ideas, but nobody changed a recurring task. Pick one real workflow and have a small team practise it together this week. Give them a reviewer and time to compare outputs. Check whether they repeat it next week without being prompted — that's the only signal that tells you whether the enthusiasm survived contact with Tuesday.

This week

Give one team one recurring task, one reviewer, and seven days. Watch for repeat use, not applause.

The invoice landed again. Nobody remembers requesting the extra seats. Somebody's still paying for them.

A licence nobody opens isn't a tooling problem, it's an adoption problem wearing a budget line. The seat itself was never the blocker — something about onboarding, workflow fit, or plain awareness is. Chasing it down usually starts with looking at who isn't using it and asking them directly, not renegotiating the contract first.

This week

Pull this month's usage report, find the lowest 20% of users, and have one real conversation with one of them about why before you touch the contract.

The pilot worked. Everyone agreed it worked. Then it sat there, because nobody owned getting it into daily use.

A successful pilot with no named owner for the next stage doesn't fail loudly, it just never boards. The people who ran it move to the next project. The result gets mentioned in a deck twice, then never again. This is the single most common place rollouts stall: not the build, the handoff.

This week

Name one accountable owner for taking this from pilot to daily use. Run through the checklist below to check whether that ownership is real or just a title.

Ownership checklist

  1. One named person, not a team or a committee.
  2. Written into their actual role, not stacked on as "additional duties."
  3. A defined handoff date from whoever ran the pilot.
  4. A way to ask for help without owning the failure alone.

Six months in, someone asks if this is working. There's nothing to compare it to, because nobody measured "before."

Without a baseline, every result is a feeling, not a finding. This isn't about building a dashboard on day one, it's about writing down four honest answers before the next thing launches, so there's something to check the result against later. It takes ten minutes and it's the difference between "I think this helped" and being able to show it.

This week

Fill in the template below for whatever's about to launch next, before it launches.

Baseline template

  1. What were you measuring before you started? (Nothing? That's the finding.)
  2. What's the one number that would tell you this worked?
  3. Who checks that number, and how often?
  4. What did "normal" look like before AI touched this process?
